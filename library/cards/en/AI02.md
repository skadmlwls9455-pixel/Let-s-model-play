If one assumes that every meaning needs a dedicated neuron of its own, reading an internal representation becomes a matter of finding name tags. However, Toy Models of Superposition shows cases in which a small ReLU model that receives sparse inputs holds more features than the number of dimensions of its representation space. When representation directions overlap, they can interfere with each other, and sparsity and nonlinear processing are involved in that trade-off. The paper also covers how the arrangement of features forms geometric structures, but it states that what generalizes unchanged to actual large models is unclear. Therefore the observation that a single neuron turns on across several topics cannot be read directly as evidence of confusion or emotion.

In this paper, superposition does not mean quantum superposition.
