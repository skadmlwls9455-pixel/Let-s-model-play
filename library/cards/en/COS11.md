In November 2026, the LHCb experiment at CERN announced the first evidence of the decay of a neutral B meson (B_d, a particle containing a bottom quark) into two muons. The rate predicted by the Standard Model is about one in ten billion. Data collected with the detector upgraded in 2024–2025 alone gave a statistical significance of 3.0σ, and combining it with the 2011–2018 data gave 3.2σ. The threshold that particle physics calls a "discovery" is 5σ, so this result is at the stage of evidence, not discovery. The observed rate agreed with the Standard Model prediction. A first hint was reported in 2015 in a joint analysis by LHCb and CMS, but it was not confirmed with data up to 2018. The analysis was led by Harry Cliff of the University of Cambridge.

Falling short of 5σ means that a statistical fluke is still possible. Just as the first hint in 2015 later disappeared, this result will firm up only as more data accumulate.
