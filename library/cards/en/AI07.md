Giving a bonus at moments when a prediction is wrong can induce behavior that seeks out new places. A prediction may be hard because there is a rule to learn, or because the thing is random by nature. OpenAI's research summary describes the noisy-TV problem, in which an agent lingers at a random screen when the next-state prediction error is used as the reward. Instead of predicting the next state itself, RND uses an exploration bonus that predicts the output a fixed random neural network produces for a visited state. This is one case of turning the word curiosity into a computable design, and it is not an experiment that measured the agent's enjoyment.

This does not mean that RND solves every problem of random environments. Curiosity here means exploration rewards and behavior, and does not determine whether subjective interest exists.
