Around the black hole in the Perseus galaxy cluster, traces of pressure waves spreading through hot gas have been observed. In this case the word sound has a physical basis: actual pressure changes in a medium. However, the released audio must not be taken as a recording made by a microphone on site. NASA explains that it extracted the waves identified in the observational data along the radial direction and synthesized them by raising the frequency by 57 and 58 octaves so that humans can hear them. Which direction is read first, which pitch range is used, and which differences are emphasized all change the listening experience.

The timbre or the eerie impression of a sonified recording is not evidence of the black hole's intention or emotion. Traces of pressure waves and the sound conversion of ordinary astronomical data are different things.
