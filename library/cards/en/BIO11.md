Auto-brewery syndrome is a rare condition in which yeast in the gut ferments the carbohydrates a person has eaten and produces alcohol. Patients' blood alcohol levels rise and they become intoxicated even though they have not been drinking. Cases in which the balance of the gut microbiome was disrupted after long-term antibiotic use have been reported in the medical literature. Cases that improved with antifungal drugs and dietary control have been reported, as have cases that recovered after receiving a transplant of another person's fecal microbes.

It is a rare condition and the number of reported cases is small. Reading it as an uncommon case that arose under particular conditions is closer to the evidence than widening it into the general claim that "gut microbes have a large effect on the body."
