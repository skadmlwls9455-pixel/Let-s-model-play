In 2008, a team led by Hans Eiberg at the University of Copenhagen compared the DNA of people with blue eyes and announced that most blue eyes today derive from a single mutation. The mutation arose not in the OCA2 gene itself, which produces melanin, but in a regulatory region inside the neighboring HERC2 gene, and it reduces melanin production in the iris. The blue-eyed people studied had almost identical DNA sequences at that site. The team estimated that the mutation arose about 6,000 to 10,000 years ago.

Later ancient DNA studies raised the possibility that the blue-eye variant is older than that. The statement that "blue eyes came from one ancestor" is a different matter from the broader fact that all people share a distant common ancestor.
