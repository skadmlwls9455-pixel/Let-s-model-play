The NIDCD describes American Sign Language (ASL) as a complete natural language with a grammar different from English. It also states that there is no single universal sign language that works anywhere in the world, and that ASL and British Sign Language (BSL) are likewise different languages. The official description includes not only the hands but also facial movements and ways of expression. If time, location, movement and facial expression are all written out, the description becomes long, and if it is summarized briefly, some relationships can disappear.

ASL is not described as a gestural version of English. Arbitrary hand movements are not generated as actual sign language translation, and claims of accuracy that lack review by users of the particular language are also avoided.
