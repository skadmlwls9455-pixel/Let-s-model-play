In 1932 in Western Australia, about 20,000 emus came to the wheat fields and ruined the crops, and at the farmers' request, soldiers of the army artillery went out with two Lewis machine guns. In the first operation in early November, the emu flock scattered and fled at the sound of gunfire, and although thousands of rounds were fired, only a few dozen birds were killed. The operation was halted after a week and then resumed, and by early December, using nearly ten thousand rounds, about 1,000 emus had been killed. The commander reported that the emus kept running after being hit by bullets, like tanks. The press called it the "Emu War," and the saying that the army lost to the birds remained. Afterward, the government responded with a bounty system instead of the army.

The saying that "the army lost" is closer to a joke made in parliament and the press at the time. The operation records note the number of emus killed as well as the failure to reduce the damage they caused.
