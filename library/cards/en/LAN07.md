The Library of Congress's description of its IRENE research states that the laterally moving grooves of a record are acquired as non-contact two-dimensional images and analyzed with software to produce an audio file. In the same project's guide, the 3-D confocal scan produces a height map of the surface of the recording medium, and it is explained that it can be applied to examine vertical-groove materials such as fragile wax cylinders. This method is not a simple magic trick in which "sound turns into a photograph." The connection is that the physical change that recorded the sound remains on the surface of the medium, and that signal is computed by reading that trace with a different sensor.

This is a story about restoring recording media on which grooves remain. It is not extended into a claim that historical sounds of a scene can be extracted from any photograph. The future research goals on the project page are not counted as achievements.
