In 1859, San Juan Island, between the United States and British Canada, was claimed by both countries as their own because the wording of the border treaty was ambiguous. On June 15, an American settler, Lyman Cutlar, shot and killed a pig that was digging up his potato patch, and the pig belonged to an employee of the British Hudson's Bay Company. The dispute over compensation grew into a threat of arrest, and the United States sent troops while Britain sent warships. Several hundred American soldiers and British forces with five warships faced each other for months, but no shots were fired. In the end the two countries agreed to occupy the island jointly, and in 1872, through the arbitration of the German Emperor Wilhelm I, the island became American territory. The only casualty of the incident was the pig.

The name "Pig War" was attached later. The British admiral is said to have disobeyed the order to land, saying he would not drag two countries into war over a quarrel about a pig.
