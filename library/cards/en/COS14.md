The Mpemba effect is the phenomenon that, under certain conditions, hot water can freeze before the same amount of cold water. Similar remarks appear in the writings of Aristotle, Bacon and Descartes. In 1963, Erasto Mpemba, a student in Tanzania, saw a hot ice-cream mixture freeze first and asked his teacher about it; together with the physicist Denis Osborne, who visited the school, he carried out experiments and published them in a 1969 paper. Evaporation reducing the amount of water, convection, dissolved gases and differences in the degree of supercooling have been proposed as candidate causes, but no single one explains it. In 2016, a research team reported that in an experiment with carefully matched conditions it could not reproduce a significant effect.

It is not the case that "hot water always freezes first." The result differs depending on how the moment of freezing is defined (when the first ice forms, or when everything is frozen), and on the container and the amount of water.
