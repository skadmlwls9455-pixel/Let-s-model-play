The Rosetta Stone carries the same document inscribed in three writing systems: Demotic, hieroglyphic and Greek. According to ARCE's account of the history of decipherment, early researchers tried to match the names of the king and queen that appear in the Greek version with the sounds on the Egyptian-script side. A name that is already known becomes a small fixed point that constrains hypotheses even without a complete translation. If the sentence order changes or a name is abbreviated, simple position matching can fail. Conversely, even when the surface differs, placing constraints such as repeated positions, length and personal names together can reduce the candidates.

The three writing systems are not treated as meaning three independent languages. The history of the Rosetta Stone's collection and ownership, and its context as Egyptian cultural heritage, are not erased either.
