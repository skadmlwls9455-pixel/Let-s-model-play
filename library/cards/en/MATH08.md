When geometry is learned only through pictures on paper, the plane that is visible can feel like the standard of space. The manuscript by Henderson and Taimiņa explains how to construct a model of the hyperbolic plane by crochet, increasing the number of stitches at a constant rate row by row. The same manuscript discusses that propositions about the embeddability of the hyperbolic plane depend on smoothness conditions and must be distinguished from physical models and approximate constructions. Therefore, calling any object an exact hyperbolic plane on the visual impression of many ruffles alone is hasty.

A finite physical model is not the same object as the ideal hyperbolic plane. The conditions of the theorem on smooth isometric embedding are not omitted in order to call it a contradiction.
