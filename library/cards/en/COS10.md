The 2006 metamaterial experiment by Schurig et al. reported that a structure surrounding a copper cylinder reduced the object's scattering and shadow under particular microwave conditions. The announcement by the institution that carried out the research explains that the device was simplified to two-dimensional rather than three-dimensional conditions, and that it was not a cloak that makes an object vanish at all visible wavelengths seen by the eye. When the wavelength, the viewing direction, and the detection criterion are left out, even a true result turns into an excessively broad story.

This is not a summary of the limits of all current invisibility research, and an abstract held by the institution was used rather than the whole of the original paper.
