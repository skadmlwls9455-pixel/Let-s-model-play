GPS satellites orbit at an altitude of about 20,000 km at a speed of about 4 km per second. According to special relativity, the clock of a fast-moving satellite runs about 7 microseconds per day slower than a clock on the ground, and according to general relativity, a clock at a high altitude where gravity is weaker runs about 45 microseconds per day faster. Combining the two effects, the satellite clock gets ahead by about 38 microseconds per day. Radio waves travel about 300 m in 1 microsecond, so without correction the clock difference alone would build up a position error of about 10 km per day. For this reason, the clocks aboard the satellites are set before launch to run slower by this difference.

The position error of an actual receiver also has other causes, such as atmospheric delay and orbit error. The figure "10 km per day without correction" is the value converted from the clock difference alone.
