Sperm whales exchange bundles of clicks called codas. A 2024 study reported a combinatorial structure that describes coda variation along the axes of rhythm and tempo plus context-sensitive rubato and ornamentation. The figure legends of the paper present several combinations that include both discrete and continuous changes. Changes according to repetition, intervals, order, and surrounding vocalizations can be observed, but they are not immediately translated into "there is food" or "glad to see you." The original abstract and figure legends were read, and the meaning of the whales' vocalizations was not directly verified.

It is not asserted that whale language has been deciphered or that it is a system of meaning identical to human language.
