A number for uncertainty has more to check than a manner of speaking does. The meaning of the number emerges only after collecting how often the answers given as 80% in similar situations were actually right. The 2022 paper Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know studied the possibility of predicting the validity of a model's own answers and the probability that it knows. At the same time, it reported that calibrating the probability of knowing was difficult on new tasks. A different study in 2024 argued that it is hard to obtain sufficient calibration from prompting alone, and presented an approach of fine-tuning using correct and incorrect examples.

Both papers were reviewed here only within the scope of their abstracts. The result of one study is not applied to all models, prompts, and fields, and the two studies are not treated as direct contradictions of the same experiment.
