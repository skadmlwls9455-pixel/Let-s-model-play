The octopus sucker is a device for gripping surfaces and, at the same time, a sensory organ that distinguishes chemical and mechanical information. The research team reported that the suckers contain distinct chemosensory cells and mechanosensory cells, and that their signal transduction properties also differ. They also presented a result that a cephalopod-specific contact chemoreceptor detects natural substances that dissolve poorly in water.

The molecular mechanism of contact chemosensation is not equated with the human experience of taste. The study species and experimental conditions are not automatically generalized to all octopus species and natural settings.
