A New Caledonian crow study presented food that could not be reached because each individual part was too short, and gave the crows objects that could be combined. Of the 8 crows that took part in the study, 4 combined the parts and made a functioning tool. In another study, crows that had experienced paper pieces rewarded according to size made pieces closer to the previously rewarded size, even in a test with random rewards and no model. Combining the two results, it cannot be concluded that "all crows draw a blueprint in their heads like humans." The first study also states that the internal cognitive process is still not clear.

These are experimental results from small samples and are not generalized to the abilities of all individuals or species. The ability to match a template is not evidence that settles cumulative culture in the wild.
