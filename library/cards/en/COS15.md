A neutron star is an object that packs 1.4 to 2 solar masses into a sphere about 20 km in diameter. It is the core left after a massive star explodes as a supernova, and electrons and protons have merged so that it consists mostly of neutrons. The density at the center reaches about 4×10^17 kg/m³, similar to the density of an atomic nucleus. At this density, an amount of matter the size of one teaspoon (about 5 ml) would weigh several billion tons if measured on Earth. NASA uses comparisons such as "a sugar-cube-sized piece with the mass of Mount Everest."

The numbers differ depending on which part of the star is meant. The outer crust is much lighter than the core. If that matter were taken out of the star, it would no longer stay at that density, so the spoon comparison is a way of converting density into Earth units.
