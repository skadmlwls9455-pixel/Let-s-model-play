The paper by Güntürkün et al. explains that whistled Turkish conveys the lexical and syntactic information of Turkish by whistling. The research team administered a dichotic listening task, presenting spoken syllables and whistled syllables, to 31 people familiar with this method. They reported that a right-ear advantage appeared in the speech condition, but no statistically significant ear advantage appeared in the whistle condition. This is not proof that "the brain is completely rewired," but a specific experiment that inferred asymmetry in language processing by using listening behavior. Even for the same content, the constraints that a medium places on temporal change and pitch can differ.

The paper's dichotic listening results are not restated as direct brain imaging or as a general law for all languages.
