In the USGS explanation, P waves are distinguished as waves that compress and expand rock in the direction of wave travel, and S waves as waves that cause shearing motion perpendicular to the direction of travel. A station's record does not contain only how strongly the ground shook. If the differences in the arrival times of P waves and S waves at several stations are read and compared with standard travel-time data, the distance from each station to the source region can be estimated and the epicenter narrowed down. In effect, space is reconstructed through time differences instead of being seen directly. If the arrival time at one station was misread, or the velocity model for passage through the Earth's interior differs, the intersection can also differ.

It is not a tool for predicting the timing or the danger of an actual earthquake, and it simplifies the velocities inside the Earth and observation errors.
