In January 897 in Rome, Pope Stephen VI had the body of his predecessor Formosus, who had died nine months earlier, taken out of the tomb, dressed in papal vestments and seated in a chair, and held a trial. A deacon standing beside the body answered in its place. When a guilty verdict was pronounced, three fingers of the right hand, which had been used for blessing, were cut off, and the body was later thrown into the Tiber. The background to the trial was a factional struggle over the Roman nobility and the imperial throne at the time. Stephen VI was deposed that summer and strangled in prison, and the popes who followed annulled the trial and reburied Formosus.

The details of the descriptions differ somewhat among later records. There is also an account that the body was recovered from the river and reburied.
