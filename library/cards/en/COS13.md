WISE 0855 is the coldest known brown dwarf, about 7.4 light-years from the Solar System. Its temperature is low enough for water to form ice clouds. In 2026, a team led by Brittany Miles of the University of Arizona observed this object with the James Webb Space Telescope for 11 hours, obtaining a spectrum every 15 minutes. They separately identified a signal of water-ice clouds thickening and thinning over time, and a signal of changing amounts of gases such as carbon monoxide and phosphine in the atmosphere below. It was announced as the first confirmation of changing water clouds on an object outside the Solar System, and the paper is to be published in The Astronomical Journal.

The observation is a single 11-hour run. The team also wrote that longer observations are needed to learn the rotation period and the three-dimensional motion of the atmosphere.
