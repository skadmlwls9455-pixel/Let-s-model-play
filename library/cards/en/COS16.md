When helium-4 is cooled below 2.17 K in absolute temperature (about minus 271 degrees Celsius), it becomes a superfluid. Its viscosity becomes effectively zero, so it flows without obstruction even through extremely thin gaps, and a vortex made by stirring does not stop. If it is kept in an open container, it crawls up the inner wall of the container as a film tens of nanometers thick (a Rollin film), passes over the rim and flows out. It was discovered in 1938 by Pyotr Kapitsa and, separately, by John Allen and Don Misener. It often appears in textbooks and experiment videos as a representative case of quantum mechanics showing itself at a visible scale.

The expression "defies gravity" is a figure of speech. The film's climb up the wall is explained by the force that makes it cling to the surface and by the property that there is no viscosity to resist the flow.
