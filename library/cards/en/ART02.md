Lenia is introduced as a family of two-dimensional cellular automata that use continuous space, time, and states and generalized local rules. The original paper classified the varied patterns found in simulation and investigated their distribution in parameter space and their forms and behaviors. The authors' official introduction lists lifelike features such as self-organization, self-repair, symmetry, and locomotion.

The paper's "species" and "life forms" are classification terms for artificial patterns. They do not automatically imply a biological species concept or the existence of subjective experience.
