Bennett's 1973 paper shows that a machine performing general computation can be constructed in a form that is logically reversible at each step. The abstract of the paper describes a construction that computes while preserving intermediate results, leaves the desired output, and then undoes the earlier computation in reverse order to clear away the unneeded intermediate results. A design that can be logically undone and a claim that an actual device operates without loss are different things. In this material, the paper's construction was read, and no machine was implemented and no energy consumption was measured.

The ideal computational structure treated in the paper does not prove a realistic lossless computer implementation. Some spelling errors from the PDF OCR were handled briefly, without correcting the quotations.
