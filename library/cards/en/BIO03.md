Polarization is a property related to the direction in which light oscillates. In octopus polarization vision research, responses to an approaching shape presented by polarization contrast rather than brightness difference were observed. Under conditions of a high degree of linear polarization, the median threshold for responding to a difference in polarization angle was 1.3 degrees, and as the degree of polarization decreased, the required angle difference grew larger. This figure is an example of how difficult it is to rank sensory ability with a single number. Mapping a channel invisible to the eye into false color turns it into data that humans can see, but that does not mean the converted display is the same as the animal's actual experience.

The median under particular conditions is not turned into "every octopus always sees 1.3 degrees." The study measured responses to sensory stimuli and did not directly read subjective visual experience.
