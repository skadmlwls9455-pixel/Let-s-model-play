When the adult (medusa) of the small jellyfish Turritopsis dohrnii is put under stress such as starvation or damage, it shrinks its body, attaches to the bottom, and can revert to the juvenile stage, the polyp. The process involves transdifferentiation, in which already differentiated cells turn into other types of cells, and because it can be repeated, it is called "biological immortality." A study published in PNAS in 2022 compared the genome of this species with that of a close species that lacks the same ability, Turritopsis rubra, and found differences in genes related to DNA replication and repair and to telomere maintenance.

It means that the animal may not die of old age, not that it does not die. It dies from predators or disease. The reverting process has been observed mainly in the laboratory, and direct observations in the sea are rare.
