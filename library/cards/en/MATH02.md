A piece that is used repeatedly and a whole pattern that repeats periodically are not the same concept. The hat research by Smith and colleagues presented an aperiodic monotile: a single kind of shape that can cover the plane yet does not permit a periodic tiling. Follow-up research treats an important condition more precisely. The hat tiling mixes reflected and unreflected pieces, whereas the modified Spectre family is presented as a strictly chiral aperiodic monotile. In the sentence "one and the same shape," whether flipping is permitted changes the actual mathematical problem. The fact that no repetition was visible on a small screen cannot prove the aperiodicity of the infinite plane.

The reflection conditions of the hat and the Spectre are not mixed. The results and conditions were confirmed from the abstracts, and the full proof of aperiodicity was not independently re-verified.
