On January 15, 1919, in the North End of Boston, a storage tank 15 m high burst and about 8.7 million liters (2.3 million gallons) of molasses poured out. The wave of molasses, about 8 m high and moving at about 56 km per hour, covered the streets, killing 21 people and injuring about 150, and destroying buildings and elevated railway structures. The tank had been built in 1915 without proper inspection, its walls were thin and its rivets weak, and warm molasses delivered the day before and a sudden rise in temperature combined with these. After several years of trial, the liability of the tank's owner, the company United States Industrial Alcohol, was recognized and compensation was paid.

Molasses was a raw material for industrial alcohol. It is a sticky liquid and might be expected to have flowed slowly, but the investigation confirmed that when it first poured out it was a fast, heavy wave.
