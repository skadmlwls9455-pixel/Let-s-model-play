In Mnemiopsis leidyi, a species of comb jelly, no anus is visible ordinarily. Only when it excretes does the end of the gut meet the outer skin and open a pore, and when excretion is over the pore closes again and disappears. In 2019, Sidney Tamm of the Marine Biological Laboratory in Woods Hole, United States, recorded this process under a microscope. The pore was formed anew at each excretion, and excretion was repeated at regular intervals. It is discussed as a comparative case in debates about how a digestive tract with separate mouth and anus arose in early animals.

"No anus" means that no open pore is observed ordinarily, not that there is no excretory passage. The observation was made in one species.
