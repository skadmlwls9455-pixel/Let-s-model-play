According to the British Museum's collection description of the Queen's Lyre, the excavators poured plaster into the spaces left by wood that had rotted away, preserving the positions of the decoration. Its present appearance was also not settled from the start. An early "harp-lyre" restoration that combined this instrument with another harp turned out to be wrong, and a new restoration based on excavation photographs was made in 1971–72. This is a concrete record that restoration is a process of conjecture and revision. Making the outward form plausible and historically proving a particular timbre can require different kinds of evidence.

The public description is material about the restoration of the outward form. This source alone does not claim that any particular modern reconstructed performance restored the exact sound of antiquity.
