In 1843, Alexander Bain of Scotland patented a device for sending pictures over telegraph lines. It is counted as the beginning of the fax. Abraham Lincoln died in 1865. Japan's samurai class had its privileges abolished over the 1870s, after the Meiji Restoration of 1868. So the 22 years from 1843 to 1865 are a period in which fax technology, the samurai and Lincoln existed in the same era. However, the first commercial fax service actually operated in 1865 between Paris and Lyon (Giovanni Caselli's pantelegraph), and Japan's first telegraph line was laid in 1869 between Tokyo and Yokohama. A telegraph line connecting the United States and Japan came even later than that.

It means that the dates overlap, not that anyone sent anything to anyone. The view closer to the sources is that it took several decades for the technology to go from a patent to an actual network.
