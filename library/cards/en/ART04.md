Turing's 1952 paper presents a system of reacting and diffusing chemical substances as a possible mechanism of morphogenesis. The paper explains that even in a system that is nearly uniform at the start, if a suitable instability exists, small disturbances can grow and produce patterns or structures. However, the fact that a mathematical model yields results resembling natural patterns does not mean that the actual developmental process of the organism concerned has been fully confirmed.

Turing's original text is a mathematical study proposing a possible mechanism. This single paper does not explain every pattern in real organisms, and it should not be generalized to claim that all diffusion creates patterns.
