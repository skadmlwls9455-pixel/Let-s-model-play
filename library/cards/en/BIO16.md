The woolly mammoths of Wrangel Island, north of Siberia, became isolated about 10,000 years ago when rising sea levels separated the island from the mainland, and they lived until about 4,000 years ago. This overlaps with the period when the pyramids of Giza in Egypt were built. A study published in Cell in 2024 read the genomes of 21 mammoths from this island and from the mainland before the isolation. It found that a population of at most about 8 animals in the early period of isolation grew to 200 to 300 and was maintained for about 6,000 years. There were traces of inbreeding, but also traces of harmful variants being gradually purged, so the team judged that inbreeding was unlikely to have been the direct cause of extinction. Humans arrived on the island after the mammoths had disappeared.

What happened at the end is still unknown. The team named a sudden environmental change or disease as candidates. The latest individual used in the analysis dates from about 300 years before extinction, so the final period is missing from the data.
