The Walters Art Museum explains that the text of the Archimedes Palimpsest was erased in the 13th century and the parchment was reused for a prayer book. The same institution's 2005 announcement reports a case in which the iron in the ink of a particular leaf was revealed by X-ray fluorescence, allowing the text beneath an overpainting to be read. Here, reading becomes a matter of distinguishing the signals that a material gives off, before it is a matter of guessing the meaning of sentences. However, the name of a technique alone does not justify saying that every page is restored in the same way. The abstract of a related RIT thesis states that the data cube analyzed was made from a small region of one page and may have limits due to noise.

This covers specific cases from the 2005 announcement and the 2006 thesis. The success rate of restoration is not generalized into a present-day achievement, and the RIT material was read only as far as the abstract.
