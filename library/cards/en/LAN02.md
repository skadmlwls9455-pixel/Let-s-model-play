Yale's Beinecke Library explains that the script and the author of the Voynich manuscript have not been identified and that the text has not been deciphered. The library provides high-resolution images of the entire manuscript for research. Even as observable material grows, explanatory candidates can keep multiplying when there are no contrasting conditions to decide the meaning. Even at the moment a symbol beside a picture that looks like a plant is called a plant name, two different inferences, "classification of the picture" and "reading of the script," are already mixed together. If any hypothesis keeps changing its rules to fit the same material, it becomes difficult to refute.

The fact that a machine produced a fluent translation does not by itself establish a decipherment. Because the expressions of the date of production within the Yale page do not agree, no specific date of production has been fixed.
