The key device of the slime mold research is that it does not confine the word memory to the inside of a nervous system. The research team observed that slime molds tended to prefer the side without the extracellular slime they had left over the side with it. In the condition where slime was on one of two branches, 39 of 40 individuals went to the clean side. In the U-shaped barrier task, 96% of the empty-floor condition reached the target within the set time, but in the condition where slime covered the whole floor and it was hard to distinguish their own trail, the figure was 33%. The researchers interpret this result as externalized spatial memory. This does not mean it is the same as human episodic memory.

The reported proportions are limited to the experimental conditions and samples concerned. Slime avoidance is not interpreted as evidence of conscious recall or emotion like that of humans.
