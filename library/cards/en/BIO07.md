Common mycorrhizal networks refer to the phenomenon in which fungal hyphal connections link the roots of several plants. But that fact and the story of "a forest internet in which mother trees send resources to look after their offspring" are not claims of the same size. A 2023 evidence-assessment paper judged that there is insufficient support for generalizations such as that the networks exist widely in forests and that resource transfer through those pathways raises seedling performance. It wrote that, as of that time, the claim of preferential transfer to offspring has no peer-reviewed published evidence. This criticism also does not mean denying all mycorrhizal connections or all resource movement.

The assessment of the 2023 review is not presented as a conclusion reviewing all follow-up studies up to 2026. A correction notice is linked to the original article, and only the scope of the PubMed abstract was used.
