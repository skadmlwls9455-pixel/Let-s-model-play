In July 1518 in Strasbourg (now in France), a woman began dancing in the street and did not stop for several days. Within a week dozens of people had joined, and within a month the number of dancers had grown to hundreds (50 to 400, depending on the record). The city authorities at first thought that having musicians play and providing a stage so that people could dance their fill would cure it, and when this had no effect, they banned the dancing and sent people to the chapel of St. Vitus. As causes, poisoning by ergot, a mold that grows on rye, and a mass psychological reaction of a society suffering from famine and disease have been proposed. The event survives in the writings of physicians of the time, in city records and in chronicles.

The accounts that many people danced to death appear in later sources, and no deaths are confirmed in the city records of the time. The cause has not been established.
