A rainbow forms when sunlight that has entered a water droplet is refracted once, reflected once from the inner surface of the droplet, and refracted again as it leaves. Light is refracted by slightly different amounts for each color, so the colors separate as the light exits. The angle at which this returning light is most strongly concentrated is about 42 degrees from the direction opposite the sun (the antisolar point), and that is why a rainbow always draws its arc on the side opposite the sun. It is the reason one can see it only when standing with one's back to the sun.

The second rainbow is light that has been reflected twice inside the droplets. It appears about 10 degrees outside the first rainbow, at roughly 50 to 53 degrees, and its color order is reversed. In the first, red is on the outside, and in the second, red is on the inside. The sky between the two rainbows is noticeably dark, and this band is called Alexander's band. It is said to have been first described by Alexander of Aphrodisias.

A rainbow is in fact a complete circle. From the ground, the horizon hides the lower part, so only the upper half circle is visible, while from a high place such as an airplane, or in the mist made by a garden hose, a round ring can sometimes be seen. The number of colors is known to be seven, which is how Newton divided them, into red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet, and it is said he also meant to match them to the musical scale. Newton at first named five, and nowadays cyan is sometimes put in place of indigo, so the number of colors is a matter of how one decides.
