In mature pine cones that respond to humidity, the scales open when the cone dries and close up when it gets wet. This movement can repeat even in a cone that has fallen from the tree. The movement is produced by a property of the material, which takes in and loses water.

The tissue layers that make up a scale expand to different degrees with moisture. As layers that are joined together change in length differently, the scale bends. The principle resembles the bending of a strip made by joining two metals with different rates of expansion, but here moisture plays the important role in place of temperature. The dead tissue of a mature cone also keeps this physical property.

When the scales open, the seeds can escape, and dry conditions favor the dispersal of seeds by wind. However, not every pine's cone opens immediately as soon as it dries. In some species there are also cones that stay closed for a long time and open with the heat of fire or in hot, dry conditions.

The opening and closing of a pine cone is not the result of guessing the rain to come. It is a response produced jointly by the present moisture conditions and the structure of the scales.
