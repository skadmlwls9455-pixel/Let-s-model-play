The idea of nothing, the symbol that marks an empty place in a numeral, and the number 0 that takes part in calculation are distinct. In 105, the 0 indicates that the tens place is empty. If that mark is removed, it becomes 15, and the value changes. On the other hand, in the calculation of adding 0 to 5, 0 itself is treated as a number.

Babylonian place-value notation also used a symbol to indicate an empty place. However, having a notation that distinguishes an empty space and having general rules of calculation for adding and subtracting 0 are different stages.

The Indian mathematician Brahmagupta, in a work of 628, dealt with rules of calculation that include 0 and negative numbers. He also wrote the rules that adding 0 to or subtracting 0 from a number leaves that number unchanged, and that multiplying by 0 gives 0. However, his explanation of the problem of dividing by 0 did not agree with today's arithmetic.

The history of 0 is hard to summarize as a single moment when nothingness was first conceived. It is a history in which the ways of writing an empty place and the rules for calculating with the number were refined over many periods.
