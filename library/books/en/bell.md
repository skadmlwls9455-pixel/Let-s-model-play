The Divine Bell of King Seongdeok was completed in December 771, in the 7th year of King Hyegong of Silla, and was hung at Bongdeoksa Temple. Its beginning goes back earlier. King Gyeongdeok began making the bell to honor the merits of his father, King Seongdeok, but could not bring it to completion, and it was finished under his grandson, King Hyegong. The Samguk yusa records the start of the work as 742. That makes almost 30 years until completion.

The bell is 3.66 meters high and 2.27 meters across the mouth, and its thickness varies from 11 centimeters to 25 centimeters depending on the part. Its weight was found to be 18.9 tons in a precise measurement by the Gyeongju National Museum in 1997. It was designated National Treasure No. 29 in 1962 and has been at the Gyeongju National Museum in Gyeongju since 1975.

At the top of the bell are the yongnyu, a dragon-shaped loop, and beside it the eumtong, a standing pipe. The eumtong is explained as a structure that aids resonance, and it is said to be a distinctive shape seen only on Korean bells.

The bell is commonly called the "Emille Bell," and a story that a baby was put into the molten metal goes with it. However, this story is not in the Samguk yusa. It appeared in written sources through the records of missionaries in the late Joseon period, and it is said to have first been recorded as a children's tale in 1925. Some claim it is a story made up by the Japanese colonial authorities and others argue against that, so its origin is not clear.
