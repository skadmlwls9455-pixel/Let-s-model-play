The song of the male humpback whale is a long vocalization in which sounds are strung together in a fixed order and repeated. Males of the same group generally share one song type while changing its form. Small changes may accumulate, or the song may change greatly to a new type passed on from another group. This does not mean that every whale species, or the whole of one ocean, sings a single tune at the same time.

A study published in 2011 reported, from 11 years of recordings in the western and central South Pacific, a pattern in which several song types spread eastward one after another. The researchers interpreted this as cultural transmission between groups. Here culture refers to behavior that is learned from other individuals and passed on.

A 2022 study reported that the detailed structure of songs passed from eastern Australia to New Caledonia was also fairly well preserved. However, similarity in recordings is indirect evidence of learning. It is not the case that who learned from whom, and where, was directly observed in every instance. Acoustic contact at shared feeding grounds and along overlapping migration routes is being studied as a candidate route of transmission.
