The proof that there are infinitely many primes appears in Book IX, Proposition 20 of Euclid's Elements. The idea is this. Lay out any finite number of primes, multiply them all together, and add 1. If that number is prime, there is a new prime that was not on the list. If it is not prime, it is divisible by some prime, but if that prime had been on the list, it would divide both the product and 1 at the same time, which is impossible. Either way, the list cannot be complete. It is often presented as a proof by contradiction, but it has been pointed out that Euclid's original proof is a direct proof that proceeds by cases.

The 17-year cicada and the 13-year cicada are periodical cicadas of North America. They spend their time underground as nymphs and emerge all at once in the same year. About twelve broods on a 17-year cycle and about three broods on a 13-year cycle are known. There are two hypotheses about why the cycles are prime numbers. One is that the years are less likely to coincide with those of predators whose cycles are shorter. The other is that mixing between broods of different cycles is reduced, and mathematical models are said to support this. Both are still hypotheses, and there is no settled answer.
