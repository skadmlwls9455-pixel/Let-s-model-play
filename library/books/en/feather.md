There is no single correct answer for the number of feathers on a bird that holds for all species. In a study of the owl Athene cunicularia in Patagonia, Argentina, the feathers of one adult male were counted as 6,148. The weight of all the feathers was 14.73 grams. This figure is a record of that specimen, and it does not mean that every individual of the same species has exactly that many feathers.

Another study, published in 2015, analyzed records of 160 birds of 85 species. Differences related to season appeared as well as differences between species. From the results of comparing different individuals at different times alone, it cannot be asserted that a single bird makes the same number of additional feathers every winter.

The role of feathers is also not just one. Flight feathers help flight, and down feathers contribute to warmth. Depending on whether the small feathers beneath the outer covering feathers were included, the comparison of different survey records can change. The species name, the number of individuals surveyed, the season, and the kind of feathers counted together determine the meaning of the number.
