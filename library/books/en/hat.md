Whether blocking the sun, keeping out the cold, or showing rank was the original purpose of the hat cannot be decided from the few surviving artifacts. What can be confirmed is that hats from different eras show different uses.

The hat found with Ötzi was made of pieces of bearskin joined into a round dome shape, and it was fastened with a chinstrap. The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology, which keeps Ötzi's clothing, explains that his attire protected the body from cold and moisture. However, it states that it is not known whether it was everyday wear or clothing for special occasions, since there is no material for comparison.

The hats of Qing dynasty officials carried other information as well. The ornament attached at the top indicated the wearer's rank. The Metropolitan Museum of Art's description of the object in its collection states that red, blue, and white ornaments corresponded to different grades.

What these artifacts demonstrate is that hats had practical functions and social markers. They do not demonstrate a single sequence in which hats developed step by step from warmth to marking status.
