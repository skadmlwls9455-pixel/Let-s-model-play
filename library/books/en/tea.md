The leaves of the drink we call tea are picked from an evergreen shrub called Camellia sinensis. Its origin is thought to be the area where southwestern China, northeastern India, and northern Myanmar meet. It is divided broadly into two varieties. The sinensis variety is used for most tea from China, Taiwan, and Japan, and the assamica variety, which has the largest leaves, is used for pu-erh and most Indian tea. Darjeeling, however, is not of the Assam variety.

What separates green tea, oolong, and black tea is how far the leaves have been oxidized. Green tea is leaf that has been neither withered nor oxidized. Oolong is withered and bruised, and then only partly oxidized. Black tea is withered, sometimes crushed, and then fully oxidized. Even with the same leaf, the color, aroma, and taste differ depending on this process and on the processing that follows.

Tea is said to be the most widely drunk beverage in the world after plain water.
