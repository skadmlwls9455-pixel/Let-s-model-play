Hangul was created in 1443 (the 12th lunar month), during the reign of Sejong, and was made known to the world in September 1446 under the name "Hunminjeongeum." Scholars hold that Sejong was deeply involved in its creation. The exact date of promulgation is not known, and Hangul Day is a day commemorating this promulgation.

For the consonants, five basic letters were made first, modeled on the shape of the speech organs when the sound is produced. ㄱ is the shape of the root of the tongue blocking the throat, ㄴ is the shape of the tip of the tongue touching the upper gums, ㅁ is the shape of the mouth, ㅅ is the shape of a tooth, and ㅇ is the shape of the throat. The remaining consonants were made by adding strokes to these letters as the sound becomes stronger.

The vowels are based on three characters: the dot ㆍ, the horizontal line ㅡ, and the vertical line ㅣ. These are explained as modeled on heaven, earth, and a person, respectively. In South Korea today, 14 consonants and 10 vowels are counted as the basic letters. The book that explains the principles and use of the letters, the Hunminjeongeum Haeryebon, was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 1997.
